The PHP vulnerabilities **recently announced** are yet another reminder of the importance of securing our Jeedom installations.

Some of these flaws are considered critical and may, depending on how they are exploited, carry significant risks.

But a question is worth asking: **why leave Jeedom directly accessible from the internet when solutions exist that make it unnecessary?**

That is precisely why I evolved my setup towards a remote access model based exclusively on **[Tailscale](/en/blog/tailscale-jeedom-acces-distant)**.

<!--truncate-->

---

## A critical vulnerability does not necessarily mean an exposed Jeedom

Jeedom relies partly on PHP. Vulnerabilities affecting the versions in use can therefore affect our installations.

There are two essential concepts to distinguish here:

- **Vulnerability**: a weakness present in a piece of software.
- **Exposure**: the ability for an attacker to reach the affected service.

A Jeedom that is publicly accessible from the internet offers more attack opportunities than an installation limited to the local network.

Even with authentication, an HTTPS certificate, and strong passwords, the web server remains reachable from the internet.

The best protection therefore also means **reducing exposed services**.

---

## Why I removed public access to Jeedom

For a long time, remote access to a home automation setup typically relied on a domain name, a reverse proxy, and port forwarding.

This architecture can be secured, but it requires ongoing monitoring and maintenance.

I chose a different approach:

- No more port forwarding on my router.
- No more public DNS to reach Jeedom.
- No more reverse proxy exposing my installation.
- Direct access from my local network.
- Remote access exclusively via Tailscale.

**My Jeedom is therefore no longer directly accessible from the internet.**

This does not mean it is invulnerable, but its attack surface is considerably reduced.

---

## Tailscale: a private network to access Jeedom

Tailscale is built on WireGuard and allows you to create a secure private network between multiple devices.

Unlike classic internet exposure, there is no need to open an inbound port on the router to access Jeedom.

Authorised devices can communicate with each other through this private network, with encrypted connections.

In practice, from my smartphone or computer, I can access my Jeedom installation as if I were at home, without making its web interface public.

And that is where the **Tailscale plugin for Jeedom** comes into its own.

## The Tailscale plugin for Jeedom

:::info Resource
[**Tailscale plugin on the Jeedom Market**](https://market.jeedom.com/index.php?v=d&p=market_display&id=4681)
:::

The goal of the plugin is to simplify the installation, configuration, and monitoring of Tailscale directly from Jeedom.

No need to run multiple Linux commands for routine operations.

The plugin provides, among other things:

- **Tailscale installation and management** from Jeedom.
- **Connection to the private network** and status monitoring.
- **Display of Tailscale IP addresses** and connection information.
- **Network route management** to reach other devices on the local network.
- **Subnet Router and Exit Node configuration** as needed.
- **MagicDNS and Tailscale SSH support**.
- **Connection monitoring**, service status, and network diagnostics.

The benefits go beyond remote access to Jeedom.

Depending on the chosen configuration, Tailscale can also provide access to other devices on your home network without multiplying port forwarding rules.

The plugin centralises these features within the Jeedom environment and simplifies their administration.

### Remote access without public exposure

The principle is straightforward:

1. Install and configure Tailscale on Jeedom using the plugin.
2. Install Tailscale on your smartphone or computer.
3. Authorise devices in your Tailscale network.
4. Access Jeedom via its Tailscale IP address or MagicDNS name.

There is then no longer any need to publish the Jeedom interface on the internet to have remote access.

:::caution Security
Authorised devices, Tailscale access rules, and account security remain essential. A private network must also be properly administered.
:::

## Updates remain essential

Back to PHP vulnerabilities.

Even with a Jeedom accessible only from the local network and Tailscale, patches are still necessary.

A compromised device, a misconfiguration, or a vulnerability in another service can always provide an entry point.

The right strategy relies on multiple complementary protections:

- Keep PHP, Jeedom, and plugins up to date.
- Minimise publicly accessible services as much as possible.
- Secure accounts and authorised devices on Tailscale.
- Check both IPv4 and IPv6 firewalls.
- Perform regular backups.

Network isolation does not replace updates, but it limits the exploitation possibilities for many network-accessible vulnerabilities.

---

## Conclusion: less exposure, more peace of mind

Critical vulnerability announcements are a regular reminder that software is never entirely free of weaknesses.

Rather than relying solely on patches and securing a public access point, I chose to remove that exposure when it was not strictly necessary.

With Tailscale, I keep remote access to my Jeedom installation and my devices, without opening inbound ports on my router.

The **Tailscale plugin for Jeedom** makes it possible to set up and supervise this solution directly from the home automation interface.

It is an approach I find particularly compelling for a personal installation.

**The best way to protect a service against direct internet attacks is often simply not to expose it there.**
